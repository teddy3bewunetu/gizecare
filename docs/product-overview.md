# ጊዜCare — Purpose, Benefits & Winning Points

**Your workday. One place.**

ጊዜCare (GizeCare) is an offline-first workday hub for freelancers, software
engineers, designers, and focused professionals. It puts time tracking,
projects, tasks, notes, documents, calendar, messaging, and everyday apps
(ChatGPT, Gemini, YouTube, and more via the in-app browser) into one desktop
and mobile workspace — so your time goes into the work, not into finding it.

**Gize (ጊዜ)** means *time* in Amharic. **Care** means protecting that time —
not only measuring it.

---

## Purpose

Most professionals run a workday across many apps: a timer here, a task board
there, notes in another window, mail and chat in two more, documents somewhere
else. Each switch costs focus. Each always-on app costs RAM, battery, and
attention.

ጊዜCare exists to **protect that time** by becoming the calm home for the
workday:

1. Make time visible (track, pause, report, know where hours went)
2. Keep daily work surfaces close (projects, tasks, boards, notes, documents)
3. Bring essential communication into the same place (Calendar, Gmail,
   Telegram, Slack, WhatsApp)
4. Keep everyday web apps inside the hub — ChatGPT, Gemini, YouTube, and any
   site via the in-app browser — without launching a separate browser stack
5. Prefer local, user-owned data over cloud lock-in

It is a **workday hub with a time-care spine** — not a timer with extras bolted
on, and not another enterprise collaboration suite.

## Benefits

### Less context-switching

Notes, tasks, calendar, mail, and chat live under one shell. You open the
workday once instead of launching a stack of apps every morning.

### Clearer time

Start / pause / resume / stop with activity awareness and idle prompts. Daily,
weekly, and monthly reports (plus CSV export) show where hours actually went —
not where you hoped they went.

### Lighter on the machine

One app instead of a dozen always-on clients means less RAM, fewer tray icons,
and a quieter desktop. The compact tracker + system tray keep the timer
reachable without a full window farm.

### Work stays on your machine

Core data is offline-first (local SQLite). Integrations serve the workspace;
they do not own your notes, projects, or time history.

### Proof and accountability when you need it

Activity percentage, interval screenshots, and exportable reports support
client billing and self-review without forcing you into a browser-only tracker.

### Capture without leaving the flow

Notebook and documents sit next to the tracker and boards — so ideas and
references stay in the same place as the hours you bill.

### Communication without a second OS

Gmail, Telegram, Slack, and WhatsApp (Web) are reachable from the same
sidebar. Calendar sync keeps the day visible.

### Media & AI apps in the same shell

Under **Apps**, ChatGPT, Gemini, and YouTube open inside ጊዜCare Browser
(sign in on the site; session kept in the webview profile). You research,
watch, and draft without leaving the workday hub — next to the tracker,
notes, and tasks instead of in a separate Chrome/Firefox window farm.

### In-app browser for everything else

Flutter chrome with tabs, bookmarks, omnibox search, settings, and Inspect.
Links from notes, mail, Slack, Telegram, calendar, and documents open here.
OAuth / `tel` / `mailto` / `tg` still use the system handler when needed.

## Winning points over other applications

Most tools optimize for **one job**. ጊዜCare optimizes for the **whole
workday**. That is the core advantage.

| Compared to… | Typical gap | How ጊዜCare wins |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pure time trackers** (Toggl, Clockify, Harvest) | Strong timers; weak or separate notes, mail, chat, docs | Time is the spine *and* the daily surfaces sit beside it — no second app for “everything else” |
| **Freelance desktop trackers** (e.g. Upwork-style memos) | Good for billable proof; often tied to one marketplace | Same compact + tray + activity / screenshot mindset, plus full projects, boards, notes, calendar, and messaging in one product |
| **Task / board tools** (Trello, Todoist, Linear alone) | Excellent boards; time and communication live elsewhere | Per-project Kanban *plus* tracker, reports, and connect surfaces in the same shell |
| **Note apps** (Notion, Evernote, Obsidian alone) | Great capture; weak native time-care and client proof | Notes and documents sit next to the timer, idle awareness, and exports |
| **Office / Google suites** | Mail, calendar, docs in the cloud; time tracking is an afterthought | Offline-first hub that *pulls in* Calendar and Gmail instead of forcing you to live in the browser |
| **Chat apps alone** (Slack, Telegram, WhatsApp) | Conversation-first; no unified workday or time spine | Chat is one pillar — not the entire product |
| **AI / media in the browser** (ChatGPT, Gemini, YouTube alone) | Useful tools, but another tab set outside your timer and notes | First-class **Apps** entries plus browser — AI and media sit beside time and work |
| **Browser + 12 tabs** | Flexible but noisy; sessions scattered; heavy on resources | In-app browser with docked webviews, bookmarks, and a focused chrome — plus native tracker and local data |

### What you are not forced to choose between

With a typical stack you often pick two:

- Accurate time **or** rich task boards  
- Local notes **or** mail/chat nearby  
- Client-ready reports **or** a light desktop footprint  

ጊዜCare’s bet is that freelancers and focused professionals should not have to
choose. **One place** means:

- **Time** — tracker, idle, activity, screenshots, reports, clock  
- **Work** — projects, tasks, Kanban boards  
- **Capture** — notebook, documents  
- **Schedule** — calendar  
- **Connect** — Gmail, Telegram, Slack, WhatsApp  
- **Apps & media** — ChatGPT, Gemini, YouTube, plus in-app browser for any site  

Time remains the spine; the other pillars exist to protect it.
